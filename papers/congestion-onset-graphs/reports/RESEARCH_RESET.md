## Candidate 1: task-specific aggregation sufficiency

**Precise question.** For a fixed history-to-message map R and the original 30-minute sustained event Y, when does keeping only R preserve event-risk prediction despite discarding sensor identities? Let I be all legitimately available 60-minute histories, masks and calendar, R=R(I), pI=P(Y=1|I) and pR=P(Y=1|R). The Bayes Brier information gap is

`Delta = E[(pI - pR)^2] = inf_f E[(Y-f(R))^2] - inf_g E[(Y-g(I))^2]`.

This conditional-expectation identity is established mathematics, not a contribution. A potential contribution would have to supply **checkable, task-specific conditions giving a nonvacuous bound on Delta for a defensible class of partially observed traffic processes**, with a way to distinguish information loss from estimation error. D=0 in a fitted graph is not such a condition.

**Falsifiable hypothesis.** A prespecified aggregate-plus-heterogeneity message can retain task risk within a 5% relative Brier loss of a full-information reference under an explicitly stated process class, with no more than a five-percentage-point loss of onset recall at the same training-selected false-alarm target. There are two different hypotheses here that must remain separate: a population information bound within that process class, and a practical comparison against an attainable fitted reference. Passing the fitted comparison cannot prove the population claim. No process class or identification argument currently makes the first hypothesis empirically testable with this archive.

**Operational relevance and difference.** A valid task-specific condition could tell an operator when sensor identity/history can be omitted from a retained regional state. It would concern what the risk query needs, not reconstruction of all local states or cheaper integration of a misspecified simulator. It would still require reading all sensors if R is computed centrally; it is an aggregation/storage question unless a different acquisition arrangement is demonstrated.

**Deployment assumptions.** Only causal histories, calendar and known sensor/graph metadata are available. Missingness must be part of the observation map. Future forcing laws and event-threshold parameters must be specified from prior knowledge or training data. A linear graph quotient is not a stochastic transition model on traffic configurations. Current-state observability, exact evolution of block means, and sufficient history for a future nonlinear event are distinct requirements. Uniform claims across unknown operating regimes are unjustified.

**Strongest simple baseline and fair comparison.** Use Stage 2 A's means, counts, calendar and heterogeneity histories as the aggregate reference, with a matched full-history learner and the same training/calibration/tuning allowance. Seasonal/persistence remains an onset diagnostic. Explicitly count reads, retained bytes, masks and evaluation cost. An equal-byte comparison would require a specified codec; the historical 13,164-byte A and 46,964-byte C arrays are not one. A calibrated forecast computed at an all-observing edge is also a necessary baseline if only one central binary query is required. An exact Bayes reference is available only in a small known-law example, not merely by naming a large fitted model “full information.”

**Smallest falsification experiment.** Before any measurements, test whether the proposed sufficient conditions imply that event probabilities agree for histories giving the same message. The two-node example below already falsifies the broad claim that exact mean closure and zero structural defect suffice. It does not falsify sufficiency of A's richer variance/extreme features. If a restricted new condition survives mathematical counterexamples, the smallest subsequent data comparison would be one frozen R, one matched learner family and an unexposed chronological panel for the unchanged task. A clear >5% loss would falsify the practical hypothesis. More failed-to-improve fitted models would not prove Delta is small.

**Required support and decision meaning.** At minimum, an independent comparison would need enough separated onset episodes and event days to assess recall as well as Brier; see the shared support requirement below. Success would need both an original, defensible condition with useful bounds and an informative independent comparison. Failure includes a counterexample within the claimed assumptions or a precise practical loss beyond the margin. Wide intervals, a weak full-information reference or unverified process assumptions mean inconclusive. The saved five-onset panel cannot decide this.

**Novelty and feasibility assessment.** Three closest primary sources are Geiger–Temmel on lumpability, Schaub et al. on equitable partitions and quotient dynamics, and Montanari et al. on functional observability. Their assumptions and exact overlap are in the [source audit](RESET_NOVELTY_AND_PROVENANCE.md). Task-specific observation is already a developed idea. Turning nonlinear future event sufficiency into a valid, useful statistical statement under dependent, partially processed measurements is a substantive unresolved obligation. The project currently has neither such a result nor credible evidence that its assumptions hold. A conditional-variance identity plus the existing fitted comparison would be a minor reframing of the failed campaign.

## Candidate 2: causal telemetry acquisition under a real budget

**Precise question and falsifiable hypothesis.** In a deployment where only 49 of the existing 325 sensors can be polled every five minutes, can a causal polling policy using already received histories reduce the original sustained-event Brier by at least 5% relative to the strongest fixed/round-robin/staleness-based schedule, at the same sensor-read and serialized-byte caps, with no more than five percentage points of onset-recall loss at the planned false-alarm target? The 49-sensor cap corresponds to the existing 15% setting; it is not optimized here. No proposed acquisition algorithm or guarantee is yet sufficiently distinct from established task-oriented sensing to justify a pilot.

**Operational relevance and scientific difference.** This would measure the value of an additional observation before it is read, with actual acquisition/communication costs. The failed campaign read all sensors for summaries and then discarded selected details. Paying for unseen values before deciding which ones matter is a materially different information problem. However, the repository documents no actual polling limit, edge capacity, latency requirement or network bottleneck that creates this problem for the present 325-sensor/five-minute feed. A convenient simulated budget is not an operational need.

**Deployment assumptions.** The scheduler can use only previous received values and masks, calendar, static metadata and its own query history. It cannot see current block statistics computed from all sensors unless those reads and messages are charged. Unpolled values remain unobserved; staleness and missingness are visible and priced. Sensor identities, request messages, timestamps, masks, packet overhead, history caching and missed deadlines count. A simulator used to evaluate value of querying would itself need validation and cannot inherit the failed model's probabilities as truth.

**Strongest simple baselines and fair resources.** Compare one fixed training-selected set, a round-robin/staleness-first schedule and a simple quantized/delta-coded message, using a common inexpensive predictor and identical development allowance. All receive the same read/byte/deadline budgets; full-read A is an out-of-budget quality reference. Selection messages convey information and incur costs. Equal history-array sizes do not establish equal transmitted bytes per time step.

If an edge computer already reads every sensor and can run the same single risk predictor, the stronger baseline is to **compute the risk at that edge and transmit its quantized probability**, with timestamp, model version and observation-quality metadata. Sensor-history transmission would then require a demonstrated extra need, such as multiple unforeseen central queries or an edge unable to compute the query. Those constraints are absent from current evidence. The elementary quantization result below explains why this baseline cannot be omitted; it is not a new coding theorem.

**Smallest falsification experiment.** First produce a deployment contract and byte/read accounting for the simple edge/schedule baselines. If the existing service has all-sensor access and edge prediction fits its budget, the need for richer central telemetry for this single query is undermined before model fitting. Under a genuine limited-read arrangement, a future minimal comparison would replay one frozen 49-read policy and the simple schedules causally, hiding unqueried inputs, on a single authorized unexposed chronological panel. No adaptive choice may see an unpolled current value. The four-state example below already falsifies a blanket diminishing-returns justification for greedy task-risk sensor selection; it is not a traffic performance experiment.

**Required support and decision meaning.** Use the shared independent event-support and uncertainty conditions below. Success requires the 5% improvement, acceptable onset recall/false alarms, actual budget compliance including overhead, and a specific contribution beyond applying a familiar selector. Failure includes being dominated by a simple schedule/edge probability baseline, relying on uncharged reads, or a precise miss of the performance margin. Sparse events, wide intervals, uncertain operational costs or a missing deployment model make the result inconclusive, not successful compression.

**Novelty and feasibility assessment.** Krause–Singh–Guestrin, Courtade–Weissman and Shao–Mao–Zhang are the three closest inspected primary sources for sensor information objectives, distributed task distortion and task-oriented edge communication. Their established results substantially overlap this direction. A distinct contribution might have to address causal acquisition, dependent event risk and real observation costs together, but that sentence is not an algorithm, theorem or new result. There is no verified deployment constraint or justified new method here. Do not launch a generic greedy-selector comparison and call it a digital-twin contribution.

## Theory obligations and bounded counterexamples

The [configuration](../configs/research_closeout.json) recorded all three questions before execution. One job ran [closeout_algebra.py](../scripts/closeout_algebra.py) at source `8a441a10dc1372af0ffe5a3bbbc10633bde773e1`. [Exact rational outputs](../results/closeout/algebra_checks.json), script hash and elapsed time are preserved. These are deliberately minimal synthetic checks, not measured traffic and not new general proofs. No seeds were searched; seed is null because the examples are exhaustive and hand specified.

**Established facts and a rejected sufficiency shortcut.** For deterministic dynamics, a future event is recoverable from a message exactly when the event functional is constant on every message fiber; stochastic risk requires equality of the conditional event laws. This is a factorization condition, not a novel theorem. Exact quotient dynamics additionally need a commuting state map and an event measurable from the quotient trajectory; reconstructing block means alone supplies only part of that condition.

Take the connected symmetric stochastic matrix

```
W = [[15/16, 1/16], [1/16, 15/16]],    x(h+1) = (1/2) W x(h).
```

There is one block, zero forcing, and D=0. Q=(1/2,1/2) gives QW=Q, so block means evolve exactly. Starting from x(0)=(-2,4) and y(0)=(1,1), both means are 1. For h=1,...,6,

```
x(h) = 2^(-h) (1 - 3(7/8)^h, 1 + 3(7/8)^h)
y(h) = 2^(-h) (1, 1).
```

Since 3(7/8)^6>1, exactly one x component but both y components exceed zero throughout these six frames. The sustained event requiring both sensors severe for three consecutive frames is false for x and true for y. The same exact mean trajectory therefore does not identify the event. This analytic construction supplies a counterexample; the script independently verifies the rational trajectories. It neither contradicts the original enclosure (its initial radius is nonzero) nor separates histories with identical A heterogeneity features. The original plan's two-cell queue example already establishes a related possibility; neither is evidence of a population information gap in PEMS-BAY.

**Rejected selection conjecture.** Let X1 and X2 be independent fair bits and Y=X1 AND X2. They can represent two persistent severe indicators over three future frames. Bayes Brier risks with no sensor, either sensor and both sensors are respectively 3/16, 1/8 and 0. Observing sensor 2 gains 1/16 alone and 1/8 after sensor 1. Thus task-risk gain fails the diminishing-returns inequality, despite a monotone event and independent measurements. Conditional independence given Y does not follow from marginal independence. A GP mutual-information or set-cover approximation guarantee cannot simply be transferred to this objective. An exact search over four states verifies this example, not the behavior of any fitted selector on traffic.

**Established posterior quantization bound.** If an all-observing edge knows the true binary posterior p, conditional expected Brier loss at q equals p(1−p)+(p−q)^2. Nearest rounding to 256 evenly spaced values on [0,1] has error at most 1/510, hence excess expected Brier at most 1/260100 = 0.000003844675125. This excludes packet overhead and assumes a true posterior. For arbitrary fitted risk q, changing it by at most 1/510 gives only the general per-outcome loss-change bound 2/510; the quadratic excess claim does not transfer automatically to fitted or merely calibrated risk. Computing a useful q remains an estimation problem. This elementary identity is a baseline argument, not a research contribution.

**What remains a candidate lemma or conjecture.** An approximate event-sufficiency lemma would need an explicit process class, treatment of partial histories/masks, control of threshold-boundary mass and accumulated model error, and identifiable constants that produce a useful bound. Generic Lipschitz-state control is insufficient for a discontinuous sensor-count event without a margin assumption. A budgeted-policy guarantee would need the correct task objective, observation protocol and cost model, and explicit dependence assumptions; universal submodularity is already false. No such new lemma or conjecture is promoted to an accepted claim by this closeout. The source audit also records statistical identification limits, so failure to reject conditional dependence is not certification of sufficiency.

## Independent support and future confirmation requirements

Neither candidate has plausible **established** independent event support in current artifacts. The only matched onset panel has five positives on five days. Full validation has 47 merged positive groups on 26 event days, all exposed. Group separation of 30 minutes does not guarantee independence; seasonality and shared daily forcing remain.

A future design would require a prospective precision calculation using development information only, with a dependence model and paired day/block uncertainty. A screening floor of 100 separated onset episodes on at least 30 event days would be more credible than five, but is **not a power guarantee**: even 100 independent binary events can give roughly ±10 percentage points uncertainty for a recall near one half. Demonstrating no more than five points recall loss or a 5% Brier effect can require substantially more support, depending on discordant alarms and day clustering. Adequate non-event days are also needed to estimate the achieved false-alarm rate. These numbers are design requirements, not new counts or a claim that the archive meets them. No new support/power computation or event scan was run.

Future confirmation would require a completely frozen representation/policy, target, baseline, learner/tuning allowance, calibration and alarm rule before opening genuinely unexposed outcomes. Training-only chronological development and calibration must retain the original 90-minute embargo and complete windows. All 36 outer validation days must be listed as exposed; a different subset, seed or split of those days cannot create independence. The existing test interval remains unopened and potentially available only after separate authorization and design freeze. Its event support is unknown and was not inspected. An insufficient confirmatory denominator would produce an inconclusive result, not permission to search regions, thresholds or datasets for a favorable outcome. A single short held-out season also cannot establish broad deployment generalization.
