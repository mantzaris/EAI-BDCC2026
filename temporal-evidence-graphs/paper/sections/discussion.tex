\section{Discussion and Limitations}

Direct checking suffices for the measured generated-answer corrections in this
bounded task. Full graph propagation matches the simpler controls on contract
errors and correction completeness, while many answers remain incomplete. The
symbolic fixture demonstrates necessary transitive maintenance; the waveform
answers usually cite operands directly, leaving little opportunity for added
benefit. A comparison can be withdrawn through its own citation even when it
also lists a parent. Future studies should prospectively vary dependency depth
and independent support while keeping candidates and answerable facts matched.

Generated parent links are declared prerequisites, not independently proven
necessary derivations. A redundant link can cause withdrawal even when direct
source checks pass. The waveform evaluation measures maintenance of accepted
declarations, not discovery of semantically necessary reasoning graphs. The
common fixture supplies the stronger test under identical, known dependencies.

The indexed relational control uses the same support predicates and agrees on
logical outcomes. Latency includes an in-process SQLite engine versus a local
Neo4j service, a small dependency structure, growing revision chains, and shared
host resources. It cannot establish a general ranking for distributed graphs,
concurrent writers, richer queries, or separately optimized deployments. One
60-second cell per method and rate supports descriptive measurements only.

The independent evaluator implements the same strict query contract as the
runtime. Its rejection of correctly labelled baseline facts restricts both
error and recall interpretation. A future protocol should distinguish useful
background evidence from misleading time attribution. Recorded-value agreement
establishes source faithfulness, not physiological truth. Lexical checks can
miss unsupported interpretations or reject equivalent paraphrases. The auxiliary
verifier shares the generator's model family, examines only 60 nonempty outputs,
and makes visible errors despite passing four simple diagnostics. There is no
human annotation gold standard or evidence of comprehensive semantic fidelity.

Controlled faults at one severity do not estimate natural fault prevalence.
Runtime has no evaluator-only corruption label. The waveform matrix fixes target
intervals while knowledge changes; historical scope and out-of-order arrivals
are correctness fixtures, not a broad evaluation of moving current-state queries.
Optional spectral summaries have no physiological accuracy validation. Ten
held-out subjects per source, two episodes per subject, related wearable hardware,
one generator, and one seed limit generalization. Subject resampling respects
dependence but cannot overcome those limits; conditional correction also depends
on what each method originally generated.

The dashboard shows consequences of review actions without certifying a supplied
correction. A mistaken rejection can remove valid content. Ranking policies,
review accuracy and speed require separate studies. Likewise, retaining useful
prior text, coalescing stale requests, and checking replacements immediately before
publication remain prospective engineering work. Here, prompt withdrawal and
complete regeneration are distinct outcomes, and the latter fails in the systems
workload.

\section{Conclusion}

The three-source evaluation finds no correction benefit from transitive
propagation over direct checking in generated waveform answers. All checked
methods satisfy the exact display contract, with limited useful coverage and
no general factuality guarantee. Full graph and relational maintenance agree on
known symbolic dependencies. Flagging succeeds at the two lower offered rates,
but the systems workload yields no complete generated replacements. The
contribution is an auditable benchmark and bounded maintenance implementation;
the results do not establish an intrinsic reliability advantage for graph storage.

and a report map results to saved inputs.
