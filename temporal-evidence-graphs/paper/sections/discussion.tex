\section{Discussion and Conclusion}

The representation distinguishes a changed value, an invalid prerequisite and a
surviving witness. Its ontology makes these commitments auditable, and its
review view exposes the relevant evidence and versions. Locality bounds candidate
re-evaluation; transitive exposure identifies required changes excluded by direct
scheduling. The two measured regimes show why declared paths and requested depth
alone cannot establish a maintenance advantage.

Grounding and useful coverage remain separate. Withdrawal can remove stale
content without restoring a required fact, and a strict contract can reject
correct background information. Independent propositions and witnesses measure
these distinctions. Admission normalization changes which supplied definitions
the checker recognizes without changing model outputs or parent links.

The conclusions concern bounded propositions, fixed witness universes and acyclic
dependencies. Recording agreement is not physiological truth. Natural faults,
clock expiry, open-ended evidence discovery and unrestricted prose need further
study. Ten held-out subjects per source, reuse of five for the extension, one
generator and one seed limit generalization; disjoint windows are not statistically
independent samples. Neo4j and the relational control agree on matched updates,
without establishing a universal storage-performance ranking. The dashboard
implements event-driven inspection and isolated review actions; human decision
accuracy, interaction latency and real-time benefit were not evaluated. The
original failure to produce adequate replacements limits claims of recovery.
