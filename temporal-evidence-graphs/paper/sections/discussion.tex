\section{Discussion and Conclusion}

A temporal evidence graph is useful when it distinguishes a changed value,
an invalid prerequisite and a surviving witness. Its ontology makes those
commitments auditable; its query projection exposes the relevant dependencies.
Locality bounds candidate re-evaluation, while transitive exposure identifies
necessary changes excluded by direct scheduling. Waveform and controlled results
instantiate both regimes: declared parents can be redundant, and requested deep
derivations need not become generated ones.

Semantic fidelity requires grounding and useful coverage. Withdrawal can improve
precision while losing a required fact; rejecting correct background can satisfy
a contract without correcting a falsehood. Independent propositions and witnesses
measure these distinctions. The separate admission replication exposes another
representation restriction without changing model outputs.

The conclusions concern bounded propositions, fixed witness universes and acyclic
dependencies. Recording agreement is not physiological truth. General prose,
natural fault prevalence, clock expiry, open-ended evidence discovery and human
review need further evaluation. Ten held-out subjects per source, reuse of five
for the extension, one generator and one seed limit generalization. Disjoint
source witnesses are not statistically independent observations.

The relational control agrees with Neo4j on matched updates; these local
experiments establish no universal storage-performance ranking. Rapid withdrawal
also does not ensure an adequate replacement. The demonstrated benefit is
selective, inspectable maintenance under explicit structural conditions.
