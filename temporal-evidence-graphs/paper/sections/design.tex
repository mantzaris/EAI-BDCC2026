\section{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:design}

\subsection{Three Sources and Participant-Level Partitions}

The synthetic benchmark generates 30-minute sessions with pulse-like cardiac
signals, respiratory oscillations, slow EDA components with response-like events,
and three-axis motion. Subject offsets and noise vary; motion and the latent
physiological-style process use separate random streams. Sampling rates are
128, 32, 8, and 32 Hz for cardiac, respiratory, EDA, and motion channels,
respectively. These are generator choices, not a validated physiological
simulator. Seeds 101--110 define development subjects and 1001--1010 define
held-out subjects. Separate seeded streams determine waveform generation,
fault assignment, and question assignment. Latent values and intervention
labels remain outside runtime prompts and validation.

WESAD provides laboratory recordings from 15 participants with chest and wrist
signals~\cite{schmidt2018,wesad}. PPG-DaLiA provides recordings from 15 participants
during daily activities~\cite{reiss2019,ppgdalia}. We inventory the original synchronized
files and retain their acquisition URLs, hashes, documentation, and stated usage
terms. Neither laboratory protocol labels nor activity annotations enter the
generator. PPG-DaLiA reference heart-rate targets and R peaks are also withheld.
The recordings supply evidence values; replay revisions do not imply that the
original investigators published erroneous measurements.

For each real source, naturally sorted subject identifiers are permuted with
NumPy PCG64 seed 20260928. Five subjects form the development partition and ten
form the held-out partition. Every episode, variant, and checkpoint from a
subject remains in that partition. The synthetic benchmark similarly has ten
development and ten test subjects. We select two target windows at 25\% and
75\% of each session's common channel duration, rounding down to a five-second
grid. Eligibility requires at least 600 seconds of synchronized coverage; all
acquired sessions qualify. The selected contexts do not overlap within a
session. Selection uses recording duration, not model performance or hidden
condition labels. Table~\ref{tab:data} is generated from the saved inventories
and held-out episode manifests.

\subsection{Windowed Features and Controlled Replays}

Features use native rates and fully observed completed windows. A 30-second
target is compared with the immediately preceding 120-second interval. Seven
overlapping update windows end at five-second increments leading to the target.
We compute EDA median and interquartile range, missing fraction, a repeated-extrema
quality cue, and acceleration-magnitude standard deviation. Wrist EDA uses
microsiemens. Synchronized WESAD acceleration uses recorded $1/64g$ units,
whereas PPG-DaLiA's synchronized acceleration uses $g$; a development alignment
check against 300 original acceleration samples verified the latter scaling.

Optional spectral summaries of cardiac and respiratory signals use a Hann-windowed
periodogram restricted to declared bands. A rate is withheld when data are
insufficient, nonfinite, or lack the required spectral concentration of 0.5.
These summaries are available context, not independently validated physiological
estimates; the required answer slots rely on directly verifiable EDA, missingness,
and motion features. Motion alone does not establish sensor failure. Numerical
extraction uses NumPy on the host, with a fixed-sample CUDA quantile cross-check;
there is no learned feature model or neural CPU fallback.

Every base episode has four paired variants:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Clean}: same-value target EDA revisions at matched times.
\item \emph{Channel fault}: either the last 15 seconds of EDA become missing or
an offset of 0.5 is added to the window. Altered arrays are separate hashed files;
derived feature versions cite their actual replay observation versions.
\item \emph{Delayed}: the target EDA feature packet arrives 60 seconds later,
while retaining its original event interval.
\item \emph{Correction}: the target median receives an explicit value revision
of $+0.5$, followed by restoration of the original value.
\end{enumerate}
Checkpoints are at 1, 31, and 91 seconds after the target window ends. Fault
and correction revisions appear at the latter two checkpoints; the delayed
packet arrives at offset 61 seconds. The clean variant follows the same
checkpoint schedule. Channel faults are split evenly into missingness and
corruption within each source. Independent seeded rankings balance question
families and fault types, with both fault types present in each source/family
group. A hidden offset is not assumed detectable before an explicit correction:
faithful reporting of the currently ingested value is not scored as omniscient
recognition of corruption.

\subsection{Questions, Conditions, and Call Budget}

Each source has five episodes in each of four question families. Current-evidence
and conflict/timing questions require the target EDA median and its signed
difference from the prior median. Sufficiency questions require the target median
and missing fraction. Revision-impact questions require the current median,
appropriate mention of a visible revision, and target motion variability.
Development and test wordings differ while retaining these fixed answer slots.
All methods receive the same current evidence serialization. For revision-impact
questions, the preceding B0 display is common context across methods; it is an
observed candidate answer, not a supplied gold answer.

Table~\ref{tab:methods} separates fresh generation from maintenance of earlier

checks and does not update old answers. B1 uses checked flat evidence and direct
reference maintenance. B2 applies those same checks over graph storage with

relational storage, respectively. All checked methods receive the same one-repair
allowance. Their fresh generations need not be identical because a fixed seed
does not remove hardware and batching effects; logical correctness is therefore
also tested with a common fixed candidate fixture.

The held-out matrix comprises $60\times4\times3\times5=3{,}600$ initial calls.
At most 2,880 validation repair calls are possible, for a hard ceiling of 6,480.
Four independent scenarios share a four-request semaphore; each scenario must
finish its current checkpoint before exposing its next one. Scenario order is
a fixed seeded permutation. Manifests, episode hashes, prompts, configuration,
runtime source hashes, and model revisions are frozen before held-out generation.
Interrupted requests with unknown outcomes are recorded rather than silently
regenerated. Resumption uses immutable case identifiers and request journals.

The final configuration uses Qwen3-8B in BF16, non-thinking mode, temperature
0.7, top-$p$ 0.8, top-$k$ 20, and seed 20260928. The input allowance is 4,096
tokens and the common output ceiling is 1,536 tokens. Development pilots exposed
truncation and constrained-decoder whitespace loops at smaller budgets; their
failures are retained. The final prompt explicitly names every required field,
and xgrammar emits compact JSON. No model weights are trained or tuned for this
study. The final pilot comprises 100 development cases and a separate 20-request
concurrency-one latency workload. Hardware and measured costs are reported with
the results.

\subsection{Independent Outcomes and Uncertainty}

The evaluator reconstructs visible versions and required answer slots directly
from immutable replay logs. It does not import runtime validation or use stored
support status as gold truth. It checks claims, evidence references, query scope,
numbers, units, dependencies, and limited sentence/paragraph consistency. A
second, separately pinned CUDA model audits 60 nonempty final explanations,
four per method/source, sampled by a fixed permutation at checkpoint 2. Four
additional diagnostic examples test the verifier. We retain prompts, outputs,
parse failures, and disagreements with exact checks. This is automated annotation
using Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct~\cite{qwen25}; common model-family errors remain possible.

For fresh answers, case error is the fraction with any incorrect displayed claim
or paragraph discrepancy. Claim error divides incorrect checkable claims by
emitted checkable claims. Required-fact recall divides correctly supplied slots
by slots answerable from the current snapshot. Useful coverage requires all
currently answerable slots in an eligible query. We report abstentions,
partial answers, parse/truncation failures, timeouts, and repair calls. Claim
error is undefined for an empty answer, and a failed or empty case remains in
the fixed case denominator and coverage metrics. Faithfulness to ingested
evidence is explicitly separate from physiological estimation accuracy.

For persistent displays, a claim requires change if it was valid when created
and becomes invalid under the current snapshot. Correction completeness counts
those claims withdrawn or revised appropriately; residual incorrect display
counts those still active. Collateral revision counts withdrawal of content
that remains valid. Initially incorrect claims are recorded separately. An
absent required-change denominator yields an undefined correction rate, not a
perfect score. Replay service times exclude offered-load queues; the systems
track measures arrival-to-completion latency separately.

Two prospective principal contrasts are M1 versus B2 for correction completeness,
and M1 versus B1 for case error jointly with required-fact recall. M1 versus B3
is a separate implementation comparison. Paired observations stay grouped by
subject, preserving all episode variants and checkpoints. First, each metric is
computed within a base episode across its variants and checkpoints; paired episode
differences are averaged within each subject. We use 2,000 paired
subject-cluster bootstrap resamples and pointwise 95\% intervals, with ten
independent held-out subjects per source. Conditional correction contrasts
include only episodes with defined denominators in both methods and subjects with
at least one such pair. We disclose both retained counts and give subjects equal
weight. Pooled descriptive rates use their displayed denominators and need not
equal the paired estimand. We report effect sizes without hypothesis-test $p$-values;
the intervals do not provide simultaneous family-wise coverage. Waveform samples,
duplicated events, and repeated prompts do not increase the participant count.

\subsection{Open-Loop Systems Workload}

The systems track gives M1 and B3 the same immutable symbolic fixture, including
an AND-dependent comparison and surviving OR support. A producer thread schedules
arrivals independently of completions at 1, 5, and 20 derived events per second,
for 60 seconds per cell. Method order alternates across rates. The target value
switches between 2 and 4 every 20 events after an initial ten-event offset.
Each implementation performs its own database retrieval and updates. A scheduled
explanation is requested every 20 events, plus a replacement when a formerly
supported claim becomes contradicted. Four requests may generate concurrently;
queues are allowed to grow and then drain.

The separate ceiling is 156 scheduled explanations, 80 affected-explanation
requests, and at most 236 validation repairs. We record arrival-to-flag time,
arrival-to-generated-replacement time, database and retrieval time, pending
explanations, event backlog, throughput including drain, and tokens. Flagging
within one second and an adequate replacement within five seconds are measured
soft targets. Replacement adequacy is initially checked against the request
snapshot; completion-time event indices permit a separate stale-answer check.
Logical assessment hashes must agree between databases for the common sequence.
These repeated symbolic events measure system load, not additional physiological
subjects or raw waveform throughput.
