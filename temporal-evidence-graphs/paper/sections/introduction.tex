\section{Introduction}

A monitoring explanation can become unreliable after it has been generated.
A delayed packet can overturn a statement that a measurement is unavailable;
a corrected observation can invalidate a numerical comparison; and a quality
notice can remove one of several sources supporting a statement. Generating a
fresh answer does not, by itself, repair an earlier answer that remains on a
screen. The system must identify which displayed statements depend on the
changed evidence and decide whether each still has sufficient support.

Consider a display containing three statements: the target electrodermal activity
(EDA) median is 2 units, its difference from an earlier median of 1 unit is 1 unit,
and an independent source also supports a value of 2 units. If the target is
corrected to 4 units, the first statement and the dependent comparison require
attention. The independently supported statement can remain. A blanket deletion
of all reachable statements loses useful content, while updating only direct
citations can leave an incorrect downstream comparison visible. The distinction
between direct checking, transitive maintenance, and alternative support is the
object of this study.

We investigate whether an explicit temporal evidence representation improves
the reliability and maintenance of generated monitoring explanations while
preserving useful answers. We separately ask whether graph storage offers an
operational advantage over indexed relational dependency tracking. These are
different questions: a storage engine can change update cost without changing
the semantics of support. A useful evaluation must therefore include both a
direct-checking ablation and a relational implementation with equivalent
maintenance capabilities.

Our experimental package uses one synthetic benchmark in two modes---exact
symbolic maintenance fixtures and waveform replay---plus recordings from WESAD
and PPG-DaLiA. The real recordings test evidence handling under two acquisition
conditions, not diagnosis or physiological causation. We evaluate 60 held-out
base episodes, four replay variants, three knowledge-time checkpoints, and five
methods. A separate open-loop workload measures update queues and the latency
of generated replacements. All generative and neural verification computation
uses a CUDA GPU; runtime checks and independent exact evaluation operate on
recorded numerical evidence.

The contribution is a controlled, auditable evaluation of evidence maintenance
for generated explanations. It combines immutable source versions, a bounded
claim contract, direct and transitive checks, persistent explanation versions,
and a strong relational control. The package retains raw model responses,
unsuccessful development runs, intervention manifests, paired case identifiers,
and scripts producing the tables and figures. We assess errors jointly with
answer coverage, distinguish retraction from useful replacement, and separate
deterministic maintenance tests from outcomes that depend on generated claims.
We do not propose a new general truth-maintenance algorithm or treat an external
dependency graph as a reconstruction of a language model's internal reasoning.

\section{Related Work}

Dependency-directed belief revision has a long history. Doyle's truth-maintenance
system records reasons for beliefs and revises their status when assumptions
change~\cite{doyle1979}. Incremental view maintenance studies how database query
results can be updated after changes to their inputs, including alternative
derivations~\cite{gupta1993}. Provenance semirings offer an algebraic account of
contributing and alternative derivations in relational queries~\cite{green2007}.
Our finite acyclic AND/OR predicates use these established ideas in a bounded
explanation task. They do not replace general database provenance semantics.
This lineage also motivates the relational control: dependency maintenance is
not exclusive to property graph databases.

GraphRAG constructs entity and community representations to support global
question-focused summarization of text corpora~\cite{edge2025}. Its evaluated
objective concerns answer comprehensiveness and diversity. Our endpoint concerns
the continued validity of previously displayed numerical statements after
evidence changes. Zep's Graphiti engine organizes conversational memory with
temporal facts, source episodes, and contradiction-based invalidation; its
evaluation includes Deep Memory Retrieval and LongMemEval~\cite{rasmussen2025}.
Temporal graphs and fact invalidation are consequently prior capabilities,
rather than novelty claims of this paper.

The HEG-TKG preprint grounds rare-disease reasoning in traceable clinical
literature and temporal disease-stage information, and evaluates citation
verification, clinician judgments, and injected-evidence counterfactuals~\cite{ahmed2026}.
It is a close example of evidence-linked temporal reasoning. Our experiment
instead uses numerical sensor replay, exact dependency fixtures, paired
coverage metrics, and a graph-versus-relational systems comparison. It neither
reproduces that clinical system nor evaluates clinical decision quality.

An evidence graph also has a limited relationship to interpretability. Turpin
et al. show that chain-of-thought explanations can omit input factors that
influence model answers~\cite{turpin2023}. Hu et al. construct semantic internal
reasoning graphs from model attribution for hallucination detection~\cite{hu2026}.
Our edges encode external evidence requirements imposed by a task contract.
Intervening on them tests traceability and maintenance behavior; it does not
establish that the model used those edges internally. Table~\ref{tab:position}
summarizes these distinctions without claiming that every capability of the
related implementations was reproduced.
