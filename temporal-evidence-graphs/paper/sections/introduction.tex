\section{Introduction}

A generated explanation can become misleading when a sensor window is corrected,
a feature fails a quality check, or a delayed observation replaces a statement
of absence. Deleting the answer discards useful information; retaining it can
preserve a wrong number. A temporal evidence graph exposes what was asserted,
which observations and prerequisites were cited, and what was known when.

A comparison may declare a parent while also citing both operands directly;
revising either operand then reaches it in one step. Conversely, a conjunction
supported only through an intermediate claim can remain displayed after a direct
checker updates that intermediate. A third case reaches a statement but preserves
it through another admissible witness. Edge semantics and support predicates,
rather than visual complexity, distinguish these regimes.

We study a researcher-specified, typed temporal property graph instantiated from
recording provenance and generated propositions. The contribution has three
parts. First, we define its construction and a query-specific semantic projection
with mappings back to stored objects. Second, we connect an explicit support
model to a locality result and a measurable condition for direct/full maintenance
equivalence. Third, we evaluate that connection using actual Neo4j exports,
independent proposition scoring, and a controlled structural extension of three
completed benchmarks. The graph exposes commitments made by the generated
answer; it is not a measurement of internal neural reasoning.

The completed waveform study supplies a useful contrast. Only
\SemParentClaims{} of \SemDisplayedClaims{} M1 displayed claims declare parents,
and direct checking matches transitive maintenance. Our structural analysis
finds zero transitive exposure in every eligible waveform revision batch. A new
protocol also distinguishes accurate background statements from violations of a
strict target-only output contract. This exposes the tradeoff between grounded
content and required-fact coverage. Finally, exact numerical programs and common
GPU-generated candidates vary dependency depth and admissible alternative
witnesses while preserving the underlying recordings. The resulting evidence
identifies when propagation matters and when simpler maintenance suffices.

\subsection{Relation to Prior Work}

Truth-maintenance systems revise justified beliefs when premises
change~\cite{doyle1979}; incremental view maintenance updates database
results from changes to their inputs~\cite{gupta1993}. Provenance semirings
distinguish jointly required inputs from alternative derivations~\cite{green2007}.
Our contribution connects these established ideas to dependencies actually
expressed by a bounded generator and independently measured maintenance outcomes.

PROV-DM distinguishes entities, activities, derivation, revision and
attribution~\cite{prov2013}. It informs our conceptual separation of recording
provenance, generated assertions and review events. The implementation remains a
property graph with Python admission checks and database identity constraints.
GraphRAG constructs entity graphs and community summaries for corpus-level
question answering~\cite{edge2025}; Graphiti, described in the Zep architecture,
maintains temporal information for agent memory~\cite{rasmussen2025}. Here the
unit of evaluation is a revisable numerical proposition and its admissible
witnesses. This distinction also separates source-grounded semantic fidelity from
the faithfulness of a model's account of its own computation, which need not
match its actual decision process~\cite{turpin2023}.
