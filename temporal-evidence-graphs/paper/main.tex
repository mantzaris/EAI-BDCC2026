\begin{abstract}
% start input ./generated/semantic/abstract.tex
Generated explanations can remain convincing after their evidence changes.
A corrected sensor measurement, for example, can invalidate an LLM's
comparison between two time windows. We present a temporal evidence graph
framework that uses Neo4j to keep generated explanations traceable and
current. The LLM proposes claims with evidence references and dependencies,
which are checked and stored alongside versioned measurements. When evidence
changes, graph traversal identifies claims to recheck. Unsupported claims
are withdrawn, while valid alternative support preserves justified
conclusions. A network dashboard makes these decisions inspectable.
Two conditional results identify potentially affected claims and explain
when direct checking misses necessary updates. We measure this gap through
transitive exposure. Experiments use synthetic streams, WESAD and PPG-DaLiA
recordings, with 240 controlled structural cases. In GPU-generated program
replays, direct maintenance misses 183 of 1,210 required changes.
Full maintenance misses none and withdraws no unaffected claims.
Exposure predicts misses in all 860 eligible replay groups.
Checked displays achieve 100\% source grounding under the numerical
protocol and 75.5--78.2\% required-fact recall. Neo4j and a relational control
produce matching outcomes. The framework connects generative AI, graph
storage and explicit support rules to make explanation revision selective
and inspectable.

\section{Introduction}

Generative AI can turn complex sensor data into explanations that people can
understand and inspect. Its value in continuous monitoring depends on keeping
those explanations connected to the evidence as measurements arrive, change
or are corrected. A useful monitoring system should identify which statements
need updating, preserve information that remains valid and make the reasons
for these decisions visible to the user.

Consider an LLM that compares two sensor windows and reports a change in skin
conductance. A later correction to one measurement may invalidate the reported
difference and other statements that depend on it. Statements supported by
unaffected measurements can remain valid. Recording these relationships makes
it possible to revise an explanation selectively and show the user how the
correction affects the answer.

We present a temporal evidence graph framework that combines LLM-generated
explanations with a Neo4j graph database and an interactive review dashboard.
The LLM receives features extracted from sensor streams and produces readable
statements accompanied by evidence references and declared dependencies.
The database connects these claims to their supporting measurements and
preserves earlier versions. When evidence changes, graph traversal identifies
the claims to recheck. The system withdraws unsupported statements and retains
those justified by surviving evidence. Labeled network views let users follow
these connections from an explanation back to its sources.

Our central contribution connects this practical workflow to a formal account
of when revision is necessary. We model how several inputs can jointly support
a claim and how an alternative evidence route can preserve it. Two conditional
results identify the region of the graph that a correction can affect and
establish when checking only immediate dependencies will miss required changes.
We introduce transitive exposure to measure this gap, connecting the graph's
structure to an observable maintenance outcome.

Experiments use synthetic streams and two real datasets, WESAD and PPG-DaLiA.
Across \SemCoreCases{} controlled structural cases, we evaluate exact programs
and dependencies actually produced by the LLM. Full propagation handles all
1,210 required claim changes in generated-program replays without withdrawing
unaffected claims. Direct checking misses 183 of these changes. Predicted and
observed misses agree in all \SemPredictionGroups{} eligible replays, and
Neo4j produces matching maintenance outcomes with an indexed relational
implementation. The waveform experiments also identify settings where direct
checking suffices, establishing when propagation provides additional value.

Independent evaluation finds 100\% grounding among checked displayed claims
under the numerical protocol, with subject-averaged required-fact recall from
\SemPpgDisplayR\% to \SemSyntheticDisplayR\%. Measuring both properties shows
how successfully the system preserves supported content and covers the requested
information. Together, the theory, experiments and network interface provide
an auditable approach to maintaining generative explanations as their evidence
evolves.




\subsection{Relation to Prior Work}

Truth-maintenance systems revise justified beliefs when their premises
change~\cite{doyle1979}. Incremental view maintenance updates database results
when their inputs change~\cite{gupta1993}. Provenance semirings distinguish
jointly required inputs from alternative derivations~\cite{green2007}.
We connect these established ideas to the dependencies a bounded generator
actually expresses and to independently measured maintenance outcomes.

PROV-DM distinguishes entities, activities, derivation, revision and
attribution~\cite{prov2013}. It informs our separation of recording provenance,
generated assertions and review events. We implement a property graph with
Python admission checks and database identity constraints. GraphRAG builds entity
graphs and community summaries for corpus-level question answering~\cite{edge2025}.
Graphiti, described in the Zep architecture, maintains temporal information for
agent memory~\cite{rasmussen2025}. Our unit of evaluation is a revisable numerical
proposition and its admissible supporting inputs. We measure whether evidence
grounds that proposition. This differs from asking whether a model's account of
its own computation faithfully describes its decision process~\cite{turpin2023}.

\section{A Typed Temporal Semantic Representation}
\label{sec:representation}

\subsection{Schema-Guided Ontology Instantiation}

The ontology is fixed in code and specifies the allowed objects and relationships.
The generator proposes instances within this vocabulary. Formally, let
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 \Omega&=(\mathcal T,\mathcal R,\operatorname{dom},\operatorname{ran},\mathcal C),\\
 G_t&=(V_t,E_t,\vartheta_V,\vartheta_E,a_t).
\end{split}
\label{eq:ontology}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal T$ and $\mathcal R$ are the node and relation vocabularies.
$\vartheta_V:V_t\to\mathcal T$ and $\vartheta_E:E_t\to\mathcal R$
assign their types. An edge of type $r$ connects allowed types in
$\operatorname{dom}(r)$ and $\operatorname{ran}(r)$. Attributes $a_t$ record
immutable and logical IDs, dataset, subject, session, event interval, ingestion
time, version, value, unit and evidence state. Constraints $\mathcal C$ require
immutable identifiers, finite acyclic declared dependencies and the admission
checks below. Ingestion time $\tau_v$ is distinct from the type maps $\vartheta$.
At knowledge time $t$, only records with $\tau_v\leq t$ are visible.
Assessments become visible at their recorded knowledge time.
Queries select the greatest version visible at their knowledge time,
using ingestion time and ID to break ties. Historical queries retain that time. Tied assessments retain their trigger IDs. Conflicting states
remain ambiguous because the timestamp does not establish the order of triggers.

Table~\ref{tab:objects} lists the stored objects. Observation windows carry hashes,
sample indices and channel metadata, while raw arrays remain outside the database.
Features carry units, extractor metadata and source IDs. The
\texttt{with\_entities} function adds subject and channel identities.
The model proposes proposition fields, evidence references, parent links and
sentences. Claims that pass the bounded checks are inserted immutably.
The Neo4j adapter adds \texttt{SupportAssessment} nodes and
\texttt{ASSESSED\_AS} edges to the storage totals.
Inserting an edge can create a placeholder for an unresolved source Record.
None occurs in the original exports. Table~\ref{tab:relations} gives relation
meanings and creation rules. Neo4j enforces unique scope/ID pairs.
Python checks record kinds, immutable payloads, acyclicity and numerical
admission. The database itself does not enforce these semantic checks.

Construction first normalizes the available evidence, then checks the model's
proposals and inserts the accepted records:
\begin{equation}
 G_t=\Gamma_\Omega(E_{\leq t},\widehat C_{\leq t})
 =\operatorname{Insert}_{\rm immutable}\!\left(
 \operatorname{Check}_q\!\left(\operatorname{Normalize}_\Omega(E_{\leq t}),
 \widehat C_{\leq t}\right)\right).
\label{eq:construction}
\end{equation}
$E_{\leq t}$ is the observation and revision log. $\widehat C_{\leq t}$ contains
proposals generated from the normalized visible evidence. Rejected proposals
remain in the request archive. New assessments and explanation versions extend
the graph without overwriting earlier content. The process instantiates the
specified ontology rather than discovering one.

\begin{samepage}
These rules explain much of the graph's topology. Each \texttt{source\_ids}
entry creates a \texttt{DEPENDS\_ON} edge from dependent to input.
Features also receive \texttt{DERIVED\_FROM} edges, and accepted claims receive
\texttt{SUPPORTS} edges in the opposite direction. Display membership creates
both \texttt{CONTAINS} and \texttt{DEPENDS\_ON} edges. Subject membership creates
\texttt{FOR\_SUBJECT} hubs. Our analysis separates these administrative,
provenance and reasoning roles.

\subsection{Edges as Explicit Semantic Commitments}

Edge types explain the declared justification. Citations offer evidence,
parent links declare prerequisites, and extraction edges record feature
origins. Membership and co-occurrence imply neither support nor dependence.
An LLM-proposed \texttt{DEPENDS\_ON} edge does not establish necessity.
\texttt{SUPPORTS} records acceptance at insertion, while \texttt{CONTRADICTS}
records a revision-triggered adverse assessment with an associated knowledge
time. Exports preserve these origins and distinguish proposed, accepted
and displayed links.

\subsection{The Query-Specific Semantic Core}

The semantic core selects an answer's claims and cited inputs for inspection
and revision:
\begin{equation}
 K_{q,t}=\pi_{q,\Omega}(G_t),\qquad
 V(K_{q,t})=C_{q,t}^{*}\cup F_{q,t},\quad
 E(K_{q,t})=D_{q,t}\cup P_{q,t}.
\label{eq:core}
\end{equation}
$C_{q,t}^{*}$ contains the latest displayed claims and their declared claim
ancestors. $F_{q,t}$ contains cited visible features and explicit availability
watches. $D_{q,t}$ contains their stored dependency edges.
When a cited feature has a newer visible version, $P_{q,t}$ traces the version
chain, retaining citation and supersession IDs. These labelled paths are
derived views, not directly stored Neo4j relationships.

Linked metadata preserves subject and sensor identities, original sentences,
versions, explanation containers and timed assessments.
Observation provenance can be expanded. Each claim's asserted fields
determine its scope.

The complete graph contains all stored records. The active view contains
the latest evidence and current displays across queries, with observation
provenance. The core focuses on one answer. It reveals which windows and
operands support a statement, which versions were known, and whether support
survives a correction. We check these questions against immutable input logs
or exact program specifications, independently of database support flags.

\subsection{Producing the Human-Review Network View}
\label{sec:reviewview}

The review view traces an answer to its evidence by expanding the semantic
core with original citations, observation provenance and visible newer
versions. For node budget $B$ and recorded times $T$,
\begin{equation}
 H_{q,t}=\operatorname{View}_B(G_t,q),\quad
 p=\operatorname{Layout}\!\left(\bigcup_{u\in T}H_{q,u};m\right),\quad
 I_{q,t}=\operatorname{Render}(H_{q,t},p).
\label{eq:reviewview}
\end{equation}
$H$ is the bounded expansion, $p$ stores coordinates and edge routes,
and $I$ is the drawing. Mode $m$ selects deterministic Graphviz \texttt{dot}
or seeded spring positioning. The spring layout uses an undirected
projection for placement only. Analysis and arrows retain the directed,
typed relationships. Time comparisons share coordinates, while records
not yet ingested remain hidden. Layout changes presentation only.
Distances represent neither semantic similarity nor neural state.

The implementation follows declared inputs within the selected database
scope. Ingestion and assessment times control visibility, while event
intervals specify when measurements or assertions apply.
Original \texttt{DEPENDS\_ON} citations remain intact. Derived paths trace
newer versions by following supersession edges in reverse.
Availability, support and display membership remain separate.
Of \SemAssessmentTies{} assessment groups sharing a record and time,
\SemAssessmentConflicts{} contain conflicting states. These remain visibly
ambiguous, with outcomes and trigger IDs preserved. Display membership
follows the saved explanation version. Evaluation uses independent
reference truth.

The node budget favors complete alternative support routes.
Omissions or incomplete groups trigger a \emph{partial view} warning
and expansion control. Identity hubs, display records, assessments and
reverse \texttt{SUPPORTS} links remain accessible in details.
Structured views place jointly required features in labelled AND
enclosures. Each row maps to a stored ID, and dashed bundles identify
the dependency edges they summarize. Different groups provide alternative
support routes.

Green, blue and grey identify claims, features and observations.
Outlines and text show revisions and support status. Arrows retain their
stored direction and labels or identify grouped edges.
Figures~\ref{fig:instances} and~\ref{fig:topology} share typed JSON views,
ID maps and rendering with the Streamlit dashboard.
Its \emph{Classic network} mode shows individual nodes
(Figure~\ref{fig:classic}). Controls select case, time, focus, route and
record. Details expose full statements, exact values, units, intervals,
versions, source IDs and assessments. Highlighting changes appearance
only, expansion reveals provenance, and review actions append events
in an isolated workspace. Readers can therefore inspect what an answer
claims, which evidence supports it and why its status changes.

\section{Support, Locality and Transitive Exposure}
\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{Support Through Admissible Witnesses}

A witness is a complete set of inputs that jointly justify a claim.
A claim remains supported when at least one admissible witness succeeds.
For its finite witness family $\mathcal W(c)$,
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Supported}(c,t;q)=
 \bigvee_{W\in\mathcal W(c)}
 \left[\psi_c(W,t;q)\land
       \bigwedge_{u\in W}\operatorname{Valid}(u,t;q)\right].
\label{eq:support}
\end{equation}
At knowledge time $t$ for query $q$, $\operatorname{Valid}$ checks input
availability, subject and time scope. Prerequisite claims must themselves
remain supported. The predicate $\psi_c$ checks the asserted interval,
selected versions, quantity, normalized unit and numerical operation.
Agreement uses $|v-\widehat v|\leq\max(10^{-6},0.01|\widehat v|)$.
A correction can therefore invalidate support while the evidence remains
available. Historical and current assertions are checked separately.
The extension's inequalities use exact floating-point comparisons.

The original model uses local AND/OR rules and two-operand differences.
Generated numerical claims require all declared parents and admissible
direct numerical evidence. Comparisons cite both operands.
The extension combines explicit AND groups and OR alternatives without
cycles. Metadata preserves these witness groups, while source IDs store
their combined inputs for traversal. Edges alone therefore cannot show
which inputs are jointly required. Claims of missing evidence require
a bounded set of possible producers and explicit availability tracking.
Absence from the graph alone is insufficient.

As in provenance and truth maintenance~\cite{green2007,doyle1979},
an alternative witness can preserve support when another fails.
Our alternatives use disjoint recording windows, providing distinct
measurement sources without assuming statistical independence.



\subsection{Locality of a Revision}

A correction identifies claims to recheck, but some may remain supported.
Let $U_\delta$ contain the revised inputs, their earlier citable versions and
changed records of evidence availability. Dependency edges point from claims
to their inputs. Traversing these edges in reverse identifies potentially
affected claims:
\begin{equation}
 R_\delta=U_\delta\cup\operatorname{RevReach}(U_\delta),\qquad
 B_\delta=\{c:S_{t^-}(c)\ne S_{t^+}(c)\},
 \label{eq:locality}
\end{equation}
Here $S_t(c)$ is the support function in Equation~\ref{eq:support}.
$R_\delta$ contains the inputs and dependents to reconsider.
$B_\delta$ contains only claims whose support actually changes.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional locality]
For a finite acyclic dependency model, $B_\delta\subseteq R_\delta$ when every
mutable input to each support predicate is explicitly represented and only
$U_\delta$ changes between the two snapshots.
\end{proposition}
\noindent\emph{Proof sketch.}
Consider nodes in order from inputs to dependents. A node outside $R_\delta$
has no changed input or prerequisite inside $R_\delta$. Its numerical inputs,
scope and availability therefore remain unchanged. Induction gives unchanged
prerequisite support and hence unchanged support for the node itself.
Including earlier versions preserves this argument for old citations, while
availability records cover changes to missing-evidence claims.\hfill$\square$

The result requires all relevant changes to be recorded. Untracked expiry,
new support routes or external rule changes can violate this assumption.
Our fixed-target queries and controlled witnesses represent changes through
version or availability events. A benign correction or surviving alternative
can leave a reachable claim supported, making $B_\delta$ smaller than $R_\delta$.

With indexed adjacency lists, traversal costs
$O(|V_{R_\delta}|+|E_{R_\delta}|)$. This excludes version resolution, support
evaluation and database access. Our structural replays also evaluate the full
small program, and Neo4j path queries may enumerate multiple paths.
The bound describes graph traversal rather than total implementation runtime.





\subsection{When Direct Maintenance Suffices}

Direct maintenance checks claims immediately linked to changed inputs.
It can miss claims that depend on those inputs through intermediate claims.
Let $A_\delta$ contain the previously admitted claims that require a state
change, as determined by an independent reference evaluation. Let $D_\delta$
contain the claims scheduled for direct checking. For $|A_\delta|>0$,
transitive exposure measures the fraction left unchecked:
\begin{equation}
 \xi_\delta=\frac{|A_\delta\setminus D_\delta|}{|A_\delta|}.
 \label{eq:exposure}
\end{equation}
We report NA when no change is required. The primary measure concerns
withdrawal of initially grounded claims. Restoration is recorded separately.
The event log and task specification determine $A_\delta$ independently
of graph paths and acceptance flags.

\begin{proposition}[Direct/full equivalence and misses]
Assume initially correct states, complete re-evaluation of $D_\delta$,
dependency semantics consistent with the reference evaluation, and no other
process revisiting excluded claims. Before fresh generation, direct maintenance
misses the fraction $\xi_\delta$ of required changes. Direct and full maintenance
agree if every claim requiring a change is directly scheduled. This condition
holds when every affected claim has a direct dependency edge to each changed
input affecting its support and all other predicates remain unchanged.
\end{proposition}
\noindent\emph{Proof sketch.}
Re-evaluation updates every required member of $D_\delta$. Excluded claims
retain their original states, so the missed set is exactly
$A_\delta\setminus D_\delta$. When all required changes are scheduled, both
methods evaluate the same support rules against the same evidence and
produce the same states.\hfill$\square$

Suppose both tests initially hold. Let $c_1$ state that test 1 holds, and
$c_2$ state that tests 1 and 2 hold, citing $c_1$ and test 2.
Changing test 1 to false invalidates both claims. Direct checking reaches
only $c_1$, giving $\xi_\delta=1/2$. Adding a direct citation from $c_2$ to
test 1 brings both claims into the schedule and reduces exposure to zero.
Alternatively, a surviving witness may preserve a downstream claim despite
the correction. Revision therefore depends on which support is lost,
as well as which claims are reachable.

\section{Evaluation Design}
\label{sec:design}

\subsection{Three-Source Evaluation}

The initial study uses synthetic physiological waveforms, WESAD laboratory
recordings~\cite{schmidt2018,wesad}, and PPG-DaLiA daily-activity
recordings~\cite{reiss2019,ppgdalia}. Each source contributes ten held-out
subjects with two episodes each. The real datasets also provide five separate
development subjects, with splits fixed using PCG64 seed 20260928.
Generation and validation use recorded features without physiological or
activity labels.

Each episode compares a 30-second target window with the preceding 120 seconds.
Four replay variants cover clean or benign updates, channel faults, delayed
evidence and numerical corrections. Evaluation occurs 1, 31 and 91 seconds
after the target window ends. Four question families cover the target EDA
median, its difference from the preceding window, the fraction of missing
EDA measurements and acceleration variability. Required facts are specified
before generation.

Five methods produce 3,600 initial cases. B0 displays unchecked outputs.
B1 checks direct dependencies in indexed records, while B2 uses Neo4j.
M1 follows both direct and indirect dependencies in Neo4j. B3 applies the
same support rules in indexed SQLite using recursive SQL queries.
All checked methods validate new answers identically and allow at most
one repair.

The initial study uses a fixed Qwen3-8B BF16 revision~\cite{qwen3}, allowing
up to three claims and 1,536 output tokens. Both studies run inference on an
RTX 5090 with vLLM structured decoding~\cite{vllm}, with CUDA execution
verified. Database operations and statistics run on the host CPU using
Neo4j Community 5.26 and indexed SQLite. Three B0 transport failures are
retained as empty outputs.



\subsection{Independent Semantic Evaluation}

We evaluate whether generated statements accurately describe the sensor data
and provide the information requested. The facts concern measurable properties,
including median skin conductance, changes between windows, missing measurements
and acceleration variability.

For illustration, suppose a question asks for the target EDA median and its
change from the preceding window. If the medians are $2.4\,\mu{\rm S}$ and
$2.0\,\mu{\rm S}$, a complete answer reports $2.4\,\mu{\rm S}$ and an increase
of $0.4\,\mu{\rm S}$. Reporting only the target median is accurate but covers
half the requested information. Reporting only the preceding median gives
correct background information without answering either requested fact.

Reference values are computed independently from saved sensor events and task
specifications. The evaluator does not use the system's support flags or an
LLM judge. Matching checks the dataset, subject, session, stated interval,
quantity, operation, normalized value and unit, evidence version, and whether
the statement concerns historical or current evidence. Citation and witness
checks are separate because a correct number can still cite the wrong source.

For generated checkable claims $C(q,t)$ and answerable required facts $R(q,t)$,
grounding precision measures factual accuracy and required-fact recall measures
coverage:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 P_{\rm grounded}&=\frac{\sum_{c\in C}\mathbf 1[\text{source-grounded }c]}{|C|},\\
 R_{\rm required}&=\frac{|\{r\in R:\text{matched by a grounded }c\}|}{|R|}.
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:semanticmetrics}
\end{equation}
The target-only answer above therefore has $100\%$ precision and $50\%$ recall.
Each required fact counts once, so repetition cannot improve recall.
The optional harmonic summary
$S=2P_{\rm grounded}R_{\rm required}/(P_{\rm grounded}+R_{\rm required})$
combines these measures. It is not conventional set F1 because generated
claims and required facts have different counting bases.
For answerable questions, empty output has precision NA and zero recall and
summary score. Fully unanswerable questions have recall and summary NA, with
missing-evidence statements checked separately. Partially answerable questions
are scored against the facts that can be established.
We score raw proposals, proposals after optional repair, accepted claims and
displayed answers separately. A limited prose audit checks literal discrepancies
and interpretive wording. To evaluate revision, we also score the same prior
answer before new evidence arrives, immediately afterward and after maintenance.
These checkpoints distinguish removing unsupported content from supplying a
complete replacement, without implying a continuous recovery curve.




\subsection{Controlled Structural Extension}

We test when indirect dependencies and alternative evidence affect revision.
Before generation, we select the first five held-out subjects per source in
natural order and both episodes, reusing 30 episodes from the initial study.
Four requested dependency depths (1--4) and two support regimes produce
240 cases.

Each case asks whether at least one eligible sensor window passes four checks
on EDA median, EDA interquartile range, acceleration standard deviation and
EDA missing fraction. Thresholds are fixed from the clean episode so all
checks initially pass. Cases provide either one supporting window or an
alternative from the disjoint preceding window. Depth one links the measurements
directly to the answer. Greater depths introduce intermediate claims about
individual tests, groups of tests and complete windows.

Exact programs implement these structures. Separately, the LLM proposes up
to 16 claims and their supporting inputs, with a 4,096-token output limit
and at most one repair. We evaluate the links actually generated, retaining
missing answers, incomplete alternatives and differences between requested
and achieved depth. Two development pilots use 40 separate calls before the
main protocol is fixed. The second clarifies readable statements, grouping
and references to earlier claims. Neither pilots nor the compiler insert
missing model links.

All methods receive the same admitted candidate, evidence updates and support
rules, with no generation after correction. B0 leaves the candidate unchanged.
Four separate updates invalidate the first test in every route, invalidate
only the primary route, make a harmless numerical correction or change an
unused cardiac feature. Neo4j and relational implementations process identical
inputs. Independent truth-table checks test whether the proposed witnesses
justify the claims and capture the required alternatives, allowing different
valid derivations. A separate evaluation of the event log determines which
claims must change.




\subsection{Graph Measurement and Uncertainty}

Read-only Neo4j exports preserve nodes, relations, assessment times, Cypher and
element IDs. Counts match the original ledger and sampled stored paths.
Separate measurements of the full graph, active graph and query core cover type mixing, degree, depth, direct citations, witness counts, reachability
and actual changes. Dependency degree excludes SUPPORTS and provenance mirrors.
Meaningful zero categories remain, and we impose no power-law or embedding
interpretation.

We first aggregate snapshots and variants within episodes, then episodes within
subjects. Pointwise 95\% intervals use 2,000 subject-cluster bootstrap resamples
with seed 20260929. Historical versions and replay copies never count as
independent participants. Detailed distributions and operational logs remain
repository data. This paper retains the definitions and primary denominators
needed to interpret the results.

\SemCoreSizeMin{} to \SemCoreSizeMax{} nodes, representing
\SemCoreRatioMin\% to \SemCoreRatioMax\% of visible storage.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}

This study demonstrates how a graph database can support reliable,
inspectable generative AI. The method connects generated statements to
their measurements, supporting claims and evidence versions in Neo4j.
These connections guide revision when evidence changes and produce
network diagrams that explain the resulting decisions. The ontology
therefore provides a common foundation for automated maintenance and
human inspection, with each displayed claim linked to a checkable
evidence history.

The real data investigation demonstrates this approach on WESAD laboratory
recordings and PPG-DaLiA daily activity recordings. Checked displays achieve
100\% source grounding in both datasets, while covering 75.8\% and 75.5\%
of required facts, respectively. The network examples make these results
concrete. A corrected WESAD measurement identifies the numerical statements
that must be withdrawn. In PPG-DaLiA, a separate recording window preserves
a valid conclusion after its primary support fails. These cases show how
the representation supports precise decisions about what changes and
what remains justified.

The theoretical and controlled results explain when this capability
requires following indirect dependencies. Across the controlled generated
programs, full maintenance corrects every required change without
withdrawing unaffected claims. Direct maintenance misses
\SemGeneratedMissed{} of \SemGeneratedRequired{} required changes.
Transitive exposure predicts the observed misses in all
\SemPredictionGroups{} eligible groups, and Neo4j agrees with the relational
control on matched updates. The waveform cases also identify when direct
checking is sufficient. This combination provides both a working method
and an explanation of the conditions under which it adds value.

The present evidence establishes reliable maintenance of bounded numerical
claims under controlled revisions of synthetic and real recordings.
Complete replacement generation, broader models and evidence discovery,
and measured benefits for human reviewers are the next development targets.
The central contribution is a tested connection between generated
language, explicit support rules and graph-based revision, making the
basis of an answer available for both automated checking and visual review.
